STANDARD SHUTDOWN PROCEDURE — FLUID CATALYTIC CRACKING (FCC) UNIT
Document reference: SAMPLE-SOP-FCC-014, Revision 3

1. PURPOSE
This procedure describes the controlled shutdown of the FCC unit for planned
turnaround or maintenance. A controlled shutdown protects the catalyst
inventory, avoids thermal shock to the regenerator internals, and leaves the
unit in a safe, isolated condition for entry.

2. PRE-SHUTDOWN REQUIREMENTS
2.1 The Shift Superintendent shall confirm the shutdown window with the
Production Planning group at least 72 hours in advance.
2.2 Confirm that the flare system has adequate capacity for the anticipated
relief load, and notify the flare operator before feed reduction begins.
2.3 Verify that nitrogen supply pressure is at least 6 barg at the unit
battery limit. Nitrogen is required continuously through steps 5 and 6.
2.4 Complete the pre-shutdown checklist and obtain sign-off from the Unit
Engineer.

3. FEED REDUCTION
3.1 Reduce fresh feed rate in steps of 10 percent of design, holding at each
step for a minimum of 20 minutes to allow the reactor temperature profile to
stabilise.
3.2 Maintain riser outlet temperature above 480 degrees Celsius throughout
feed reduction. Below this temperature, incomplete vaporisation may cause
liquid carryover into the reactor.
3.3 When feed reaches 30 percent of design, begin reducing catalyst
circulation in parallel with further feed cuts.

4. CATALYST CIRCULATION AND REGENERATOR
4.1 Stop fresh catalyst addition once feed is below 30 percent.
4.2 Burn off remaining coke in the regenerator, holding the dense bed
temperature between 660 and 700 degrees Celsius until flue gas carbon monoxide
falls below 200 ppm.
4.3 Do not exceed 730 degrees Celsius in the regenerator dense bed at any
point. Exceeding this limit risks permanent damage to the catalyst and to the
cyclone refractory.

5. STEAM PURGE AND COOLING
5.1 Cut feed completely and immediately establish steam to the riser and
stripper.
5.2 Purge the reactor and regenerator with steam for a minimum of four hours,
then switch to nitrogen.
5.3 Cool at a maximum rate of 30 degrees Celsius per hour. Faster cooling
risks thermal shock and refractory spalling.

6. ISOLATION AND HANDOVER
6.1 Isolate the unit with double block and bleed on all hydrocarbon lines.
Install blinds at the battery limit and record each blind on the blind list.
6.2 Purge with nitrogen until hydrocarbon content is below 1 percent LEL at
all sample points.
6.3 The unit may not be released for entry until atmospheric testing is
complete and a confined space entry permit has been issued.
6.4 Record final conditions in the shift log and hand over to the Turnaround
Coordinator.
