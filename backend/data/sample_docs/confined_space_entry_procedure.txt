CONFINED SPACE ENTRY PROCEDURE
Document reference: SAMPLE-SOP-HSE-021, Revision 5

1. PURPOSE
This procedure defines the controls required before any person enters a
confined space. Confined space entry is the highest risk routine activity
carried out on the site, and the majority of fatalities in similar incidents
across the industry involve either an untested atmosphere or an attempted
rescue by an unprotected colleague.

2. DEFINITION
A confined space is any enclosure with limited openings for entry and exit,
unfavourable natural ventilation, and which is not designed for continuous
occupancy. This includes vessels, columns, tanks, sewers, pits, and excavations
deeper than 1.2 metres.

3. ENTRY PERMIT
3.1 No person shall enter a confined space without a valid Confined Space
Entry Permit signed by the Area Authority and the Entry Supervisor.
3.2 The permit is valid for one shift only. It must be re-validated at the
start of each shift and after any break in work exceeding two hours.
3.3 The permit shall be displayed at the point of entry for the duration of
the work.

4. ISOLATION AND PREPARATION
4.1 The space shall be isolated from all sources of hazardous energy and
material by double block and bleed, or by physical disconnection and blinding.
Valve closure alone is not acceptable isolation.
4.2 The space shall be drained, purged and ventilated before testing.
4.3 All blinds shall be listed on the blind register and verified in place by
the Entry Supervisor.

5. ATMOSPHERIC TESTING
5.1 Test the atmosphere before entry and continuously during occupancy.
5.2 Acceptance criteria for entry are: oxygen between 19.5 and 23.5 percent by
volume; flammable gas below 5 percent LEL; hydrogen sulphide below 5 ppm; and
carbon monoxide below 25 ppm.
5.3 Test at multiple depths. Heavier-than-air gases accumulate at the bottom of
a vessel and a reading taken at the manway may not be representative.
5.4 If any reading falls outside the acceptance criteria, all persons shall
evacuate immediately and the permit is void.

6. ATTENDANT AND RESCUE
6.1 A trained attendant shall remain stationed at the entry point for the
entire duration of the entry, and shall not enter the space under any
circumstances.
6.2 The attendant shall maintain continuous communication with entrants and
keep a written log of every person entering and leaving.
6.3 Rescue equipment, including harness, retrieval line and breathing
apparatus, shall be positioned at the entry point before entry begins.
6.4 In an emergency the attendant shall raise the alarm and initiate the
rescue plan. Under no circumstances shall the attendant attempt an unassisted
entry rescue.
